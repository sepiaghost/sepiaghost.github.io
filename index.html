01 · privacy

Differential Privacy for Genomic Data Sharing

Quantifies the privacy-utility tradeoff in genomic data release: implements the Laplace mechanism across seven privacy budgets (ε = 0.1–10) on real RNA-seq data, then measures both how much statistical accuracy is lost and how well a simulated membership-inference attacker is actually defeated at each level.

ε 0.1–10privacy levels tested
~0.50attack accuracy under noise (vs. 1.0 unprotected)
F1 < 0.06DE-gene recovery, entire tested range

The headline finding: privacy against re-identification is cheap to buy — even light noise collapses attacker accuracy to chance — but preserving genuine scientific utility under that same noise is hard, and doesn't improve smoothly as the privacy budget relaxes.

03 · biophysics

Hodgkin-Huxley Neuron Model

A numerical implementation of the Hodgkin & Huxley (1952) model of the action potential in the squid giant axon — four coupled nonlinear ODEs, solved with an adaptive solver (deSolve::ode(), lsoda) rather than the fixed-step Euler's method used in the original hand-computed version.

A personal recreation: this started as an undergraduate biophysics assignment built by hand in Excel, assigned by the professor who is now writing one of my Ph.D. recommendation letters. Rebuilding it in R extends the original with an F-I curve sweep to locate rheobase and a double-pulse protocol to measure the absolute refractory period.

04 · deep learning & privacy

Differentially Private Deep Learning for Cancer Classification

Extends the differential-privacy work from a single released statistic to a full trained neural network: a tumor vs. normal classifier on TCGA-BRCA gene expression, trained with DP-SGD (via Opacus/PyTorch) across a sweep of privacy budgets and benchmarked against a non-private baseline.

1,231samples, top 2,000 variable genes
98.8%baseline accuracy, no privacy
ε 0.5–20DP-SGD privacy budgets tested
~0.998AUC held across the full ε range

Rather than a clean textbook tradeoff curve, accuracy fluctuates non-monotonically across epsilon values while AUC stays consistently high throughout — evidence that per-run training variance, not privacy budget, dominates at this scale, and that DP-SGD preserves near-baseline discriminative power even under tight privacy constraints.
